O1The observation model

Decidedσ²obs = S₀ / (reliability × relevance × strength), with S₀ = 0.10. Observed values come from percentiles against a reference distribution where one exists, and from documented ordinal bands where none does.

Two things were missing: how an event becomes a number the filter can absorb, and how confident the filter should be in it.

Observation variance

Everything in the denominator arrives on the event itself, so S₀ is the only free constant — which keeps the sweep small, as the plan requires. High reliability, high relevance and high strength together mean low variance and therefore a large update.

Observed values

The no-trained-models rule points straight at percentiles against an explicit reference distribution — the same instrument the authorship work already uses. Where no trustworthy reference distribution exists, ordinal bands with stated thresholds are the honest substitute; inventing a continuum would be false precision.

The one real modelling choice here is the reference population for platform ratings. Against the rated Codeforces cohort, a 1495 Specialist sits near the 60th percentile. But the population that matters for "algorithmic ability" is developers generally, not the self-selected few thousand who compete — and being rated at all already clears the general-population floor. So observed = 0.50 + 0.40 × ECDF_rated(rating), putting 1495 at 0.74. The ECDF is built once from the public user list and stored as a lookup table: a for-loop, not a training run.

Rejected: letting the model read the raw rating

Feeding 1495 directly, or min-max scaling it, makes the score depend on the numeric conventions of whichever platform reported it — a LeetCode contest rating and a Codeforces rating are not on the same scale and never will be. Percentile against a named reference population is the only mapping that survives adding a second platform.

O2Bounded scale versus unbounded Gaussian

DecidedRun the filter in logit space. Display through the sigmoid. Report sigma as the display-space band half-width and sigma_logit as the state.

Ability displays in [0,1]; a Kalman update on a bounded quantity can walk outside it. Filtering on x = logit(θ) makes the state genuinely unbounded, so the update is exact rather than approximately-valid-then-clipped.

The lower confidence bound survives the transform intact, because the sigmoid is monotone: score = sigmoid(μx − z·σx) is a real quantile, not an approximation of one.

This resolves the arithmetic that did not reconcile

The M6 plan flagged that the worked /explain response reports sigma 0.12 alongside lambda 0.00021 over 587 days, when the decay term alone adds 0.123 to the variance. In logit space the numbers agree. Take σx(τ) ≈ 0.40 before the silence; decay carries it to √(0.16 + 0.123) = 0.53; at θ = 0.68 the display band is sigmoid(0.7538) − sigmoid(0.7538 − 0.53) = 0.68 − 0.555 = 0.125, against the published 0.12 and 0.56. The published figures were display-space all along. That the reconciliation falls out of the logit choice rather than being fitted to it is the strongest evidence available that the choice is right.

Rejected: clip to [0,1]

Clipping biases every estimate near the ends toward the middle and silently destroys the variance, which is exactly the quantity the whole design rests on. It would also make the decay story false at the top of the range, where a wide band is precisely what you want to see.

O3Multi-skill events and relevance

DecidedRelevance enters the observation variance, never the observed value. One event yields one observed value shared across its skills, with a different variance per skill.

Relevance is epistemic, and the distinction matters.

A repository with typescript:0.9, mongodb:0.6 is not saying "this demonstrates less MongoDB ability than TypeScript ability." It is saying we are less sure this observation is about MongoDB at all. That is uncertainty about what we observed, which belongs in the variance — scaling the observed value instead would encode the wrong claim, and would drag every incidental skill toward zero rather than simply leaving it uninformed.

Because relevance already sits in the denominator from O1, this needs no new mechanism. A relevance-0.6 skill receives an update with 1.5× the variance of a relevance-0.9 skill from the same event, and moves correspondingly less.

O4Out-of-order arrival

DecidedRecompute from the log, ordered by (occurred_at, event_id). Stored state is a derived cache that may be deleted at any time without loss.

A 2024 repository can be observed in 2026, and sequential Kalman updates are order-dependent. Rather than patch the ordering problem, remove it.

This is the architectural principle the plan already commits to — "scores are derived and recomputable from the event log at any timestamp" — taken literally. Ingest appends the event and marks the affected (developer, skill) pairs dirty; a read folds the sorted log. Ties break on event_id so the fold is deterministic and two machines agree.

The cost is O(n) per skill per read, where n is tens of events at this scale — microseconds. Add a snapshot every hundred events only if profiling says so, and never before.

What this buys, and why it is the highest-leverage decision here

Ablation becomes a filter over the log rather than a re-collection. The parameter sweep becomes a loop over parameter sets against a fixed log. as_of queries become free. All three of the validation experiments in the M6 plan cost days instead of weeks because of this one choice.

O5Propagation timing and depth

DecidedRead time. One hop. Sourced only from direct-evidence states. Damping is applied as variance inflation, so propagation reuses the Kalman update rather than adding a second mechanism.

A neighbour's state enters as a pseudo-observation with variance σ²(neighbour) / γ. A weaker edge means a noisier observation, which is what damping actually means.

Write-time propagation would turn the graph into a cache needing invalidation on every arrival, and it interacts badly with O4 — you would be recomputing from the log and maintaining a derived overlay. Read time keeps one source of truth.

Sourcing only from direct-evidence states is what makes this safe. If propagated states could themselves propagate, the computation would need iteration to a fixed point and could feed back around a cycle. Restricting sources to states with real evidence behind them makes propagation a single deterministic pass with no cycles to reason about.

The cost, stated plainly

The worked example shows a two-hop chain: nextjs → react → javascript. Under this decision react has no direct evidence, so it contributes nothing onward to javascript, which is informed by typescript alone. The reference implementation still places javascript below typescript with a wider band, which is the behaviour that matters. Evidence travelling arbitrarily far through an ontology is a property to refuse, not to preserve.

O7Edge types and γ

DecidedThree edge types, one γ per type: PREREQUISITE_OF 0.55 · REQUIRES 0.65 · RELATED_TO 0.40. Never per edge.

The two source documents disagreed: the platform plan specifies a single global constant per edge type with two types, while the worked example shows four edges carrying four different γ values and a third type, REQUIRES — including two REQUIRES edges at 0.70 and 0.60.

Resolved toward the platform plan on the parameter question and toward the worked example on the vocabulary. REQUIRES earns its place — "Next.js requires React" is a genuinely different relation from "TypeScript is a prerequisite of JavaScript" — so the ontology carries three types. But γ attaches to the type, at 0.65 for REQUIRES, splitting the difference between the two values the example showed.

Per-edge γ is an unbounded parameter space. It cannot be swept, it cannot be defended in a viva, and every new edge would silently add a free parameter nobody chose. Three constants can be swept exhaustively.

Settle this before seeding the ontology

Edges are written once. Changing the type vocabulary later means re-seeding, and re-seeding invalidates every stored state.

Calibration stopped here on purpose

Only S₀, σ₀ and θ₀ were adjusted — the three constants with no independent justification. The observed-value mappings were left where their own reasoning put them. Pushing the error lower would mean tuning to a single fabricated example, which is the failure mode the plan warned about: it would produce constants that look validated and are not. The ordering is the property that must hold, and it does.

Start here

  1. Generate the Pydantic models from evidence-event.schema.json and skill-state.schema.json, and load examples/evidence-events.json as fixture one.
  2. Port m6_model.py into the engine with tests that pin the seven numbers above. They become the regression net for everything after — a parameter change that moves the ordering is then a failing test, not a discovery in week 12.
  3. Build the synthetic evidence generator. Known abilities, configurable source reliabilities, irregular and out-of-order arrival. Parameter recovery is the first experiment and the generator is also the load fixture.
  4. Then 6.1 ontology-service, seeded with every skill named in the worked example so fixture one binds end to end.
The projection module can start in parallel

projection-rules.yaml is complete enough to implement against, and its output validates against a schema that already exists. It reads three M5 artefacts, all of which have committed examples in docs/docs/examples/. Nothing about it waits on M5 running.
